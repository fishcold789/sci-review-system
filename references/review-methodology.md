# Review methodology and research protocol

Select the review method before broad searching or prose drafting. The method
controls the research question, eligibility rules, search record, screening,
appraisal, synthesis, and reporting claims.

## Review types

| Type | Use when | Minimum protocol |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `narrative` | Explain a field, mechanism, history, or debate with flexible searching | scope, search rationale, source selection, synthesis limits |
| `systematic` | Answer a focused question with reproducible comprehensive retrieval | eligibility, databases, date, screening, appraisal, flow record |
| `scoping` | Map concepts, evidence types, and gaps in a heterogeneous field | broad PCC-style scope, search, charting, gap map |
| `critical` | Evaluate assumptions, methods, controversies, or evidence quality | explicit critique criteria and counter-evidence |
| `methodological` | Compare methods, protocols, metrics, or implementation choices | common comparison dimensions and applicability limits |
| `meta-analysis` | Combine sufficiently comparable quantitative estimates | effect definition, variance, heterogeneity, bias, sensitivity |
| `meta-synthesis` | Integrate qualitative or conceptual findings | coding, interpretive method, disagreement and reflexivity |

If the user has not chosen a type, propose one with a reason, alternatives,
consequences, and a human confirmation point. Do not call a review systematic
because it has many references. Do not claim PRISMA, registration, or a
meta-analysis unless the protocol and required records actually exist.

## Research protocol fields

Record research question, review type, population/material/system, phenomenon or
intervention, comparator, outcomes, context, date/language limits, databases,
query versions, eligibility, screening roles, appraisal method, synthesis plan,
deviations, and reporting guideline. A protocol may be revised, but each change
must have a decision record and scope version.
